* Decision summary

[[file:0006-engineering-runtime-and-library-boundaries.org][RFC 0006]] refines the current ownership and runtime direction: Yuanxu shared
mechanisms, Lab product mechanisms, Aitia SDLC assurance, DE engineering Library
and Rust execution. [[file:../adrs/0009-rust-engineering-runtime-and-DE-library.org][ADR 0009]] accepts that direction while cross-runtime
admission, authorization and recovery remain proposed and unqualified.

Lambda Aitia organizes its software-engineering capabilities around the
complete SDLC, including requirements, design, algorithms and implementation
strategies, verification/testing, delivery, operation, maintenance and
retirement. Its POO-native assurance kernel models why an engineering claim is
justified, what defeats or invalidates that justification, which obligations
follow, and which decisions/effects must remain blocked. Software semantics
belong to Aitia's modules; generic graph, temporal/causal and runtime mechanisms
remain Agentic AI Lab-owned, and existing verifiers retain their checking semantics.

The core abstraction is an executable causal assurance case, not a collection
of verification result flags:

claims, assumptions, typed relations, obligations and evidence, together with
invalidation, decisions and effect guards.

[[file:0004-sdlc-design-and-causal-trajectories.org][RFC 0004]] refines this architecture with design alternatives, conditional
guarantees, behavior and design-evolution trajectories, reusable Recipes and
the deferred MRR direction. Software design is an early leverage point within
the full lifecycle. The eight-stage assurance/execution flow is a protocol
used throughout that lifecycle, not a complete SDLC phase list.

* Problem

Conventional pipelines retain =test passed=, =proof passed= or =review passed=
without retaining enough structure to answer whether the result still applies
after a source, assumption, dependency, tool or policy revision changes.  They
also tend to blur several incompatible relations:

- a value was derived from a source;
- one component structurally depends on another;
- one event caused or enabled another;
- evidence supports or defeats a claim;
- a decision authorizes an effect.

Collapsing these into one edge type permits invalid inference.  Aitia therefore
models relation planes separately and admits cross-plane projections only
through named, checked functions that emit receipts.

* Semantic objects

All public values are ordinary Gerbil POO objects or nominal boundary values.
Pure functions derive indexes, closures, plans and projections.

** Artifact

An immutable or revisioned input/output with identity, media kind, digest,
revision, owner and provenance.

** Claim

A scoped predicate with assumptions, possible defeaters, support requirements,
validity interval, revision and current status.

** Assumption

An explicit premise.  It is never hidden in an adapter configuration.  An
assumption has an owner, scope, revision and review/expiry policy.

** Observation and Event

An Observation binds a fact to a clock role, logical position and source.  An
Event binds identity, subject, event kind, observation, payload, modality,
commitment state and declared causal parents.

** Obligation

A question that must be discharged for a claim or decision at an exact
snapshot.  The obligation states the required evidence kind and capability;
it does not select or invoke a tool by itself.  A candidate obligation is
explicitly bound to the snapshot identity, revision and canonical context
digest before selection.  That context digest excludes only the obligations'
own binding fields to avoid a self-referential hash; the final snapshot digest
includes the complete bound obligations.  Rebinding an old obligation is not
replacement-obligation derivation and is rejected.
The first derivation step emits an inert replacement-requirements receipt
from an old snapshot, a target snapshot and a change set.  It names affected
old obligations and their structural witnesses, and blocks automatic carryover
when the source binding, target frontier, claim or policy is not current.
It does not assign a new obligation identity or claim a replacement exists.
An explicit second step may bind a caller-authored fresh obligation already
declared in a target draft with the revised claim and =supports= edge.  It
replays the derivation, rejects non-canonical or unresolved snapshots, and
returns a final snapshot plus an inert declaration receipt.  A changed claim
blocks automatic carryover but not this explicit declaration; a policy change
still blocks it.  Neither step admits evidence or authorizes an effect.

** Evidence

An attributable result binding producer, tool/version, input artifacts,
obligation, output digest, subject, revision, scope, validity interval and
admission status.

** Finding and Counterexample

A typed challenge to a claim, model or assumption.  It remains visible after
repair and is linked to the evidence that closes or supersedes it.

** Decision and Effect

A Decision records a policy outcome over an exact assurance snapshot.  An
Effect is a separately identified real-world operation.  A decision can
authorize an effect request; it does not prove that the effect started or
completed.

** ADR and RFC relationship

An RFC frames a broad problem, architecture, invariants and open questions.  An
Architecture Decision Record closes one bounded architectural choice with its
context, drivers, considered options, outcome, consequences and confirmation.
Typed =refines-rfc= and =implements-rfc= links connect the two without merging
their lifecycle or identity.

The =modules/ADR/= record model follows the Nygard core and MADR review fields.
Its lifecycle is forward-only: =proposed= may become =accepted= or =rejected=;
an accepted record may become =deprecated= or =superseded=.  Supersession
creates reciprocal links and new revisions while preserving both predecessor
values.

An accepted ADR says that maintainers selected an architectural option.  It
does not say that evidence supports the option at the current source snapshot,
that an assurance obligation is discharged, or that an effect is authorized.
=ADR->assurance-decision= therefore projects accepted/proposed records to
=unknown=, and deprecated/superseded records to =stale=.  Only a separately
admitted assurance closure and authority receipt can cross the effect boundary.

Requirements, assumptions, alternatives and conditional guarantees form the
design context for these choices. A finding can reopen that context before
implementation changes. Algorithm optimization is evaluated against the chosen
contract; changing its assumptions or guarantees reopens the design decision.
The existing design-contract review checks declarations, not arbitrary logical
implication. RFC 0004 specifies the corresponding qualification work.

* Relation planes

** Provenance plane

Representative relations: =derived-from=, =generated-by=, =cites= and =used=.
Provenance establishes lineage.  Activity usage plus generation does not by
itself manufacture a derivation or causal claim.

** Structural dependency plane

Representative relations: =depends-on=, =composes=, =implements=, =tests= and
=refines=.  =composes= points from a composite claim to a component claim;
its reverse impact projection uses the shared Agentic AI Lab Graph.
This plane supports reverse impact and obligation selection.  Its output is
explicitly structural and has no release or runtime authority.

** Temporal and causal plane

Representative relations: =precedes=, =causal-parent=, =enables= and
=prevents=.  Modalities are observed, declared, derived, hypothesized and
counterfactual.  Missing parents and temporal contradictions create an unknown
frontier.  Earlier-than never automatically promotes to caused-by.

Agentic AI Lab's Temporal Causality owns generic event graphs, causal cuts, trajectory
assessment and witness paths. Aitia connects scoped system behavior to the
evolution of design decisions and their assurance using checked projections.
Late observations create new assessment cuts; observed failures, model traces
and counterfactual branches retain their modality and environment. Full Aitia
trajectory integration is a pending RFC 0004 gate, beyond structural impact.

** Assurance plane

Representative relations: =supports=, =discharges=, =defeats= and
=invalidates=.  Assurance edges bind exact claims, assumptions, obligations and
evidence revisions.

** Authority plane

Representative relations: =authorizes=, =denies= and =requires-review=.
Authority consumes an admitted assurance closure but is neither an assurance
edge nor a causal edge.  Authority is revocable and snapshot-bound.

* State model

The core does not force truth into a Boolean.  The initial status vocabulary is:

| State | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| supported | current admitted support closes the scoped obligation |
| violated | current evidence contradicts the required predicate |
| unknown | required facts or relations are unresolved |
| stale | a bound revision, interval or tool/source identity no longer matches |
| conflicted | attributable current inputs disagree without a resolution rule |
| hypothesized | candidate material not admitted as observation |
| counterfactual | explicit alternate branch, never committed history |
| not-applicable | an accountable policy decision excludes the obligation |

No state silently upgrades to =supported=.  Resolution functions return a
typed result with diagnostics and witness paths.

* Snapshot and invalidation

An assurance snapshot binds:

- graph identity and canonical digest;
- source and artifact revisions;
- claim and assumption revisions;
- immutable event cut and unresolved frontier;
- policy identity/revision;
- admitted evidence identities and validity intervals.

Change processing is pure:

#+begin_export mermaid
flowchart TD
    C["Change<br/>admitted"] --> W["Structural witnesses<br/>computed"]
    W --> A["Claims and assumptions<br/>affected set selected"]
    A --> S["Dependent support<br/>invalidated"]
    S --> O["New obligations<br/>pending"]
    S --> D["Dependent decisions<br/>invalidated"]
    D --> E["Effect guards<br/>blocked"]
#+end_export

An invalidation receipt reports affected and unresolved identities.  Its slots
for temporal assessment, assurance closure, release authorization and runtime
execution are false unless a separately owned phase supplies the corresponding
receipt.  Presentation values cannot mutate the admitted snapshot.

* Functional verification planning

The planner consumes =Change=, =Snapshot= and =Policy= values and returns a
=PlanningResult= value.

PlanningResult contains ordered obligations, capability requirements,
dependency constraints, rejected alternatives and blockers.  Tool invocation
is outside this function.  An adapter later consumes one obligation and returns
a typed execution outcome; evidence admission is another separate function.
The first reported-outcome contract records exact input identity, revision and
digest bindings and emits an inert structural eligibility receipt. A reported
success, even when structurally eligible, is not a trusted execution
attestation. Host-owned adapter identity and effectful admission are required
before an =AssuranceEvidence= may become authoritative. Its caller-supplied
=admission-state= is metadata, not proof of that transition. Changing that
slot after verification would alter the snapshot context and invalidate the
outcome's original binding. A configured Host therefore issues a separate
admission object over candidate evidence without editing the snapshot. Its
private issuance record binds the verifier seal, obligation, evidence, outcome,
full semantic snapshot, validity interval and source-invalidation epoch.
Currentness is checked against the Host's configured snapshot source and clock;
the Host reconstructs the source snapshot to reject stale displayed digests,
and source changes invalidate earlier seals, including if the old cut reappears.
This is a Host-local admission candidate, not a decision or effect grant.
=assurance-host-admitted-support?= consumes that exact current admission with
one current =supports=/=discharges= assurance relation to its bound obligation.
It requires the relation to be present in the same Host snapshot and rejects
copied admissions, speculative modalities, expiration and source invalidation.
This is one edge witness; it does not close all claim requirements or produce
an =allow= Decision. Full closure, Cedar policy and Runtime effect gating remain
separate steps.
=assurance-host-required-support= now evaluates the claim-declared obligation
inventory against the Host's current cut. Its typed POO receipt reports one
blocker per required obligation, rejects missing obligation-to-claim and
evidence-to-obligation links, and leaves empty/duplicate requirements,
assumptions, defeaters, unresolved frontiers and challenges visibly blocked.
Even =complete?= means only that the declared support inventory is witnessed;
=release-authorized?= remains false. Validity intervals, policy evaluation and
the actual Decision authority boundary are still separate work.
The Host may now issue a weak-key-indexed Decision preflight for an exact
*undecided* Decision node already present in that cut. It checks the
=depends-on= edge to the supported claim, policy/snapshot/subject bindings and
current required support; currentness rechecks the Host source epoch and the
admissions. Copies, revocation, source changes and rollback cannot reuse it.
This preflight carries =release-authorized? #f= and is not a Cedar result,
an =allow= Decision or an effect token. The Agentic AI Lab Cedar Provider constructs
inert inputs; the independent Runtime Host still owns policy evaluation and
effect admission.
=assurance-host-cedar-request= now rechecks that exact preflight and projects
its Decision authority, claim subject, policy identity/revision and full
semantic snapshot digest into Agentic AI Lab's Cedar request family.
The application supplies the action, intent and Agentic AI Lab runtime handoff.
The resulting request and its context are editable input, not a second Host
seal; copying or changing them cannot establish Aitia admission. The Runtime
Host rejects the projected =agentic_ai_lab_preflight_only= marker before policy
evaluation, regardless of its value. This fail-closed check blocks accidental
promotion of the official preflight path; it does not attest an edited request
with the marker removed. A future Runtime-owned admission must independently
bind the source cut at use, evaluate Cedar through both engines and gate the
effect. The optional Cedar preflight adapter keeps a weak identity ledger for
its projected requests. =assurance-host-cedar-request-current?= rechecks the exact issued
request and the current source cut, rejecting copied or edited presentations,
revocation, source change and rollback. The configured Scheme Effect Host calls
this source predicate before and after its policy callback. Its configured
policy reader checks the requested policy identity and revision against
the policy and entity digests of the permit. It checks the same binding at
reservation and delivery. A detected policy change retires the grant, including
after the old policy returns. The grant binds the handoff sequence, payload
byte count and payload digest as well as the separate intent digest.
The Scheme lifecycle suite refused revision, policy-digest and entity-digest
substitutions and rejected delivery after a policy replacement. Restoring
the old policy left the old grant retired. A newly issued grant delivered

=assurance-host-sealed-support?= is the configured Host seal predicate, not a
promotion transition.
The lower-level =assurance-support-sealed-under-adapter?= is conditional on a
caller-supplied adapter and time; it is not an authority or effect gate.

evidence/outcome bindings and a Agentic AI Lab verification seal over the complete
semantic subject. Agentic AI Lab rejects copied or forged adapter presentations as
well as copied, modified, expired or revoked receipts. This proves issuance by
the exact adapter instance, not that a caller-supplied adapter was installed
by a trusted Host. Adapter selection and the evaluation clock must both be
controlled at the execution boundary; caller-provided values cannot authorize
an effect. Cedar and Runtime Host remain separate.
Verifier choice explanation consumes the inert plan and an explicit catalog of
POO-native candidate declarations.  Evidence-kind/capability compatibility and
declared numeric priority determine a deterministic choice; every rejected

declaration is not proof that an adapter is installed, executable or trusted.

Verifier integrations include Lean proof, Quint/TLC model check, runtime replay,
unit test, integration test, Cedar differential, static analysis and human
review.  This list is extensible through named POO-native helpers, not raw
compute hooks.
Tool-specific producers qualify against the existing evidence-kind vocabulary;
the maintained lane does not include TLAPS.
Reuse the necessary upstream checker and trusted execution boundary before
adding a new implementation, as specified by RFC 0004.

* Support assessment

The [[file:../../dev/sysml/aitia-commit.sysml][SysML v2 AssuranceSupport
package]] models a judgment over the existing Claim, Obligation, Evidence
and Relation inventory at a specific semantic snapshot. The linked Cases
implement the scoped assessment below. Unresolved assumptions, defeaters
and challenges keep the claim open.

| Judgment | Question | Owner |
|----------+----------+-------|
| Admission | Did the recorded method actually consume the bound input and produce this outcome for this obligation? | Assurance Host with independently observed Rust execution and ORG Contract validation |
| Support | Which fragment of the scoped proposition does admitted evidence establish, under which conditions? | Scheme Assurance over the current graph, with ASCENT deriving support, contradiction and missing relations |
| Closure | Do current support and explicit dispositions cover every required obligation for the declared scope? | Assurance on the claim's declared inventory |
| Authorization | May this principal perform this exact effect under current policy? | Governance and its effect boundary |

An assessment carries claim and obligation references, the observed fragment,
method and strength, subject and scope, assumptions, defeaters, residual
obligations, shared input or oracle dependencies, and a validity binding.
Strength is method-specific. A Lean theorem, a bounded Quint check, a
named integration test and an operational observation have different
applicability. They can complement one another when their correspondence
is established. Shared input or oracle dependencies qualify any claim of
independence.

Assumption dispositions bind an owner, scope, current supporting evidence
and validity condition. An unresolved defeater keeps the corresponding
obligation open. Residuals can remain visible outside a narrower supported
claim. Accepting a residual risk is a scoped Governance decision and leaves
the associated unproved proposition visible. A changed source, method,
provider guarantee, projection or environment reopens affected obligations.
Use the full semantic snapshot when dependency coverage is uncertain.

** Residual disposition

An outstanding engineering obligation may point through =requires-review=
to a Governance Decision for the same subject and claim. The Decision
binds the exact snapshot revision and current policy. A separate obligation
with =human-review= and capability =residual-risk-acceptance= supports that
Decision. Its owner-recorded outcome must come from the named authority
and bind the canonical Decision digest. This review obligation sits outside
the engineering claim's support requirements.

The Host projects a =residualDispositions= row for a current accepted
review. It reports the open obligation, Decision, authority, policy
revision, admission and outcome digest. The engineering obligation
remains in =remaining=. Source changes, stale admissions and review
withdrawal remove the current disposition. Governance still owns effect
authorization and the execution boundary checks it independently.

The Host consumes the configured review ledger. Connecting a deployed
Governance authority as that ledger's issuer remains an integration step.

** Assumption review

The Host implements the =human-review= disposition for a named Assumption
in the current snapshot. The Assumption and claim use the same scope. A
required obligation must declare =human-review= with capability
=assumption-review= and link to the Assumption with =supports=. The exact
Host admission must carry a recorded outcome from the Assumption owner.
The Host checks both recorded outcome custody and the current input
binding. The condition assessment identifies the review obligation and
admission, or gives the reason it remains open. Withdrawal or expiry of
the review admission reopens the condition. Absolute dates on Assumptions
remain open until a clock with the appropriate validity semantics is
available. The assessment carries no effect authority.

** Agent input delivery boundary

The linked Rust owner serializes the projected provider history once and
asks the Assurance Module to compare its ordered fragment with the
owner-supplied source history. The fragment retains tool call identity,
name and arguments, paired output identity and content, and system and
developer constraints. Ordinary user and assistant messages and
reasoning items are outside this judgment. A missing pair, duplicate
call, unsupported item or changed required fragment refuses delivery.

On acceptance the Rust entry invokes the consumer with the same
serialized packet it sent to Scheme for checking. It returns no
reusable permission or checked flag for a different packet. This
entry requires the caller to supply authentic source history. The
recorded-source entry reads the history file from the selected JJ tree
and rechecks the exact workspace cut before invoking the consumer.
A successful callback produces a delivery observation with the JJ cut,
repository-relative history path, source digest and exact delivered packet
digest. A failed callback returns its failure without a delivery observation.
The owner checks the cut again before using the observation as current
evidence. A rewrite during the callback preserves its result and the
historical observation. The currentness check refuses the old cut.

The local linked Rust integration exercised both entries with the compiled
Scheme program. It delivered byte-identical accepted input and refused
missing calls, missing or reordered outputs, duplicate calls, omitted or
weakened constraints, and a substituted output. The recorded-source Case
used JJ v0.47.1. A dirty checkout edit did not affect the read. After a
rewrite, the old cut and a projection of the former constraints were both
refused. The test independently recomputed the source and packet digests
from the original recorded history and the bytes received by the consumer.
It exercised a callback failure and a JJ rewrite inside a successful
callback. Neither supplied current evidence. The successful callback
still returned the exact bytes it received.

The linked delivery operation records the application-declared project
with the exact jj cut before invoking the consumer. The linked
=assess_recorded_agent_input= operation accepts the expected project and
workspace, the Rust-owned delivery observation and the exact packet. It
matches the resolved workspace root against the recorded cut, rereads
history and checks currentness again after assessment. The Assurance Host
materializes the history and packet and checks their digests and the
declared fragment before admitting the observation. The report identifies
the project as claim subject, along with the commit, history path, method,
witness and remaining requirements. The delivery observation retains the
exact packet digest. The operation uses a Host scoped to the assessment and
returns a report without an admission capability.

The linked test obtained one witness with both inputs and no remaining
fragment obligation. The report granted no release authority. A different
packet that satisfied the same fragment rule was rejected because its
digest differed from the delivered packet. A different project in the

* Assurance laws

The architecture requires executable formal statements of at least these laws:

1. Evidence freshness: support for revision R uses evidence bound to R and to
   the exact subject/assumption closure.
2. No stale authority: an impacted dependency invalidates dependent authority
   until current obligations are discharged.
3. Modality preservation: hypothesized/counterfactual events cannot discharge
   an observed-fact obligation.
4. Counterfactual isolation: an alternate branch cannot become committed
   history without an explicit authorized transition.
5. Composition obligation: independently supported A and B do not imply that
   compose(A,B) is supported.
   An explicit =composes= relation identifies the component claims, while a
   separate obligation must be declared on the composite claim, named in that
   claim's support requirements, linked by =supports= and bound to the exact
   snapshot.  The composition-requirements receipt only checks this structural
   declaration; it never admits evidence or grants authority.
6. Unknown blocks closure: an unresolved required frontier cannot grant
   authority.
7. Effect separation: authorization neither proves effect start nor effect
   completion.

Lean proves general laws. Quint/TLC checks lifecycle interleavings such as a
source change racing a cached decision or an effect beginning after revocation.
The bounded lifecycle model additionally separates safety from progress:
eventual effect start is required only when the source and clock remain stable,
no revocation intervenes, and the Host weakly fairly schedules every enabled
stage. Without that scheduling assumption, a stuttering behavior after a grant
is a valid counterexample. Neither result substitutes for a trusted Host clock,
adapter identity, or Runtime effect gate.

* Public API direction

The intended module family is =modules/assurance/=.  It follows the established
five-role convention:

- =types.ss=: nominal boundary predicates and status/relation kinds;
- =objects.ss=: POO prototypes and immutable constructors;
- =funs.ss=: pure validation, indexing, closure, invalidation and planning;
- =config.ss=: declared default policies only;
- =interface.ss=: the sole supported public re-export boundary.

No public raw alist schema, generic edge constructor, implicit global registry,
import-time I/O, verifier invocation or action hook is accepted.

* First Software/SDLC qualification

Aitia owns Software Engineering and SDLC vocabulary, standards, requirements,
lifecycle facts and GitOps sources.  Its assurance kernel is domain-neutral:
NASA and provider-specific facts enter only through typed projection boundaries.
The reference qualification projects only the identities and relations required
by this RFC, then exercises:

- structural impact without causal promotion;
- declared, observed, derived, hypothesized and counterfactual separation for
  software changes and lifecycle decisions;
- explicit unknown frontier;
- evidence invalidation after a source revision;
- new obligation planning;
- stale release/effect decision rejection at Cedar/Runtime Host boundaries.

Success establishes a bounded software lifecycle reference case, not general
standards compliance, release certification or unrestricted causal inference.

* Rejected designs

1. One generic knowledge-graph edge type: rejected because relation families
   have incompatible inference and authority rules.
2. A fixed CI pipeline: rejected because verification is derived from impacted
   obligations and available capabilities.
3. Copying Software/SDLC standards into the assurance kernel: rejected because
   standards already have an Aitia-owned SDLC module and hard-coding them into
   the kernel would collapse the domain/module boundary.
4. A second Graph implementation: rejected because Agentic AI Lab owns the graph
   substrate and algorithms.
5. Green-check aggregation: rejected because it loses revisions, assumptions,
   provenance, invalidation and effect boundaries.
6. Runtime fallback authorization: rejected because missing authority must stay
   a typed failure.
