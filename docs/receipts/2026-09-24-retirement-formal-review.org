* Decision and evidence boundary

New persistence integration is deferred. The prior
[[file:2026-09-24-retirement-impact.org][Scheme Impact checks]] exercise structural/temporal analysis, not a proof of
retirement safety. Existing Lean laws covered fixed-snapshot impact and design
substitution; the existing TLA+ model covered assurance admission/authorization.
Neither covered the new retirement review. This slice adds design experiments,
not deployed deletion, a new public DSL, or a second generic Impact engine.

[[file:../plans/engineering-case-source-inventory.org][The source inventory]] distinguishes Temporal's documented cancellation
semantics, the etcd watch report and a LangGraph recovery report from our own
proposed transfers. None was replayed locally in this slice. Known upstream
reports are not new bug discoveries, and these models do not reproduce their
implementations.

passed their expected-outcome contracts. This means 17 configurations within
three unittest methods, not 17 successful safety theorems. The harness checks
the exact counterexample property, exit code, nonempty exploration and trace.
Negative checks stop at a counterexample and need not exhaust their queues.

One local work-only run took 2.595 s; retirement-only took 3.321 s; the final
all-module run took 11.390 s. These are single-run unittest wall times including
JVM startup, not a repeated benchmark or a general speedup claim. Atomic
selection avoids unrelated models; changing the shared work module still
requires its retirement consumer check.

Lean 4.31.0 built both assurance/SDLC libraries. Six new exported laws prove
initial safety, one-step preservation, reachable-state safety and three unsafe
state witnesses. All 16 audited laws reported no axioms; no =sorryAx= was used.
The observation relation overapproximates all nonterminal observations, while
the finalize action checks current work/inventory. This is an abstract-model
proof, not an established refinement from TLA+ or Scheme.

The invalid module selector =not-a-module= was also checked with the project's
existing Python interpreter: argparse exited 2 rather than running zero tests.
The same unittest suite remains discoverable through the existing CI command.
No remote CI run or clean-checkout validation is claimed.

The inspected grammar requires these expressions/declarations on one line;
TLC's parser accepts the native multiline forms. These are first-failure
locations, not an exhaustive parser capability audit: named =INSTANCE= and
qualified operators also require qualification after the newline gap is fixed.
No alternate parser, flattened-source workaround or false roundtrip receipt
was introduced. Native parser acceptance/byte-roundtrip remains failed.

* Next gates, not claimed complete

Follow-up: [[file:2026-09-24-retirement-ingress-review.org][the ingress review]] adds a bounded challenge to item 3 and extracts the
existing grant predicate. The source hashes and 17-case results above remain
the earlier cut; the follow-up records its changed runner/model hashes and
22-case run. Items 1, 2 and live authority correspondence remain open.

1. Qualify the native parser for modular/multiline TLA+ without weakening the
   source-byte identity or introducing a downstream parsing authority.
2. Bind admitted Scheme Contract identity/revision and projection digests to
   the TLA+ and Lean views, with an executable correspondence check.
3. Challenge the terminal assumption by composing retirement with ingress and
   revocation: a late request after finalization must not be hidden by a
   frozen state. Reuse the current authority owners; do not add a policy engine.
4. State fairness assumptions before proving eventual drain/retirement.
   Unknown inventory, replay, multiple consumers and repeated late arrivals
   remain outside this finite model's complete guarantees.
5. Keep persistence and real effect execution deferred. Neither abstract proof
   nor the synthetic Scheme case constitutes production readiness.
