* Context and Problem Statement

The migrated ADR module described lifecycle policy but had no nominal record,
option, consequence or link model.  It could not prove which option was chosen,
why a decision changed, whether supersession was reciprocal, or how the record
related to an RFC and an assurance decision.

* Decision Drivers

- Stay compatible with widely used ADR document structures.
- Preserve machine-checkable state transitions and immutable history.
- Join bounded decisions to broader RFCs without conflating the two artifacts.
- Prevent an accepted document from silently granting effect authority.

* Considered Options

1. Keep only the historical governance Profile and GQL query.
2. Store ADRs as untyped Markdown/Org files and infer lifecycle from Git.
3. Add POO-native ADR records based on the Nygard and MADR structures, with
   typed RFC links and a non-authorizing assurance projection.

* Decision Outcome

Choose option 3.  =modules/ADR/= owns nominal records, options, consequences,
links, lifecycle validation, reciprocal supersession and canonical projection.
Org documents remain the review surface; POO values are the executable semantic
surface.  RFC links use =refines-rfc= or =implements-rfc= rather than pretending
that an ADR replaces the RFC.

* Consequences

- Good: required rationale and rejected alternatives are inspectable.
- Good: forward-only lifecycle and reciprocal supersession are executable.
- Good: RFC, assurance and authority remain separate typed boundaries.
- Bad: legacy ADR documents require normalization before projection.

* Confirmation

- =just test-contribute lambda-aitia ADR= validates records, negative cases,
  transitions, reciprocal supersession and assurance projection.
- =just test-contribute lambda-aitia assurance= validates the receiving nominal
  Decision type and its independent support rules.
- Source and registry tests require the exact uppercase =ADR= module namespace.
