* Context and Problem Statement

The original job-executor declaration says that the provider deduplicates
=tenant/job/operation= keys and guarantees at most one commit *per key*. A
real-process qualification case exposes the gap: after a provider commits but
its acknowledgement is lost, a restarted worker can generate a new key. The
provider correctly deduplicates each key while committing the same logical
job operation twice. This is a known fault-injection case, not a newly found
defect in a deployed application.

* Decision Drivers

- Keep a single logical identity across retries and process restarts.
- Preserve completion after a transient acknowledgement loss.
- Do not claim exactly-once effects without provider idempotency and durable
  task-state assumptions.
- Keep a revised guarantee and its evidence separate from the old declaration.

* Considered Options

1. Keep the per-key guarantee and accept duplicate logical operations.
2. Turn off retries, avoiding duplicates by sacrificing completion.
3. Persist or deterministically derive a stable provider key from the logical
   operation identity and revise the guarantee scope.

* Decision Outcome

Propose option 3. The job ledger owns the logical operation identity and retry
state. The same tenant/job/operation key reaches an idempotent provider after
restart. The revised =unique-effect= guarantee is at most one external commit
per logical job operation across retries and restarts, conditional on the
provider retaining its deduplication record. A changed guarantee, retry
variation and ADR identity reopen verification; old evidence is not admitted
by declaration-level compatibility.

* Consequences

- Good: acknowledgement loss does not create a second effect in the bounded
  qualification case, and the worker can still complete after restart.
- Bad: key retention, scoping and provider deduplication are new operational
  dependencies to qualify before any production assurance claim.
- Neutral: this proposed ADR neither issues a Host seal nor authorizes an
  effect. Acceptance requires independent review of the actual provider and
  persistence mechanism.

* Confirmation

- The Python project's installed-wheel qualification runs two worker processes
  against a persistent SQLite ledger and a loopback HTTP provider. The buggy
  key strategy fails with two commits; the stable strategy passes with one.
- The Scheme design-replacement test treats the changed guarantee and decision
  as requiring fresh review, with no evidence admission or release authority.
- Full source-bound Host admission and production provider qualification remain
  open.

* Historical input and current coverage

Revision r2 retains the proposed decision and adds an applicability and owner
audit. It does not accept the ADR or certify a production provider.
[[file:../plans/engineering-case-source-inventory.org][The case-source inventory]] records the AWS article used as design inspiration,
alongside candidate history/observability and composition cases. Its treatment
of late retries and changed intent motivates questions about retention and
payload binding; it is not verification evidence for this project.

Source inspection on 2026-09-24 found that the fixture worker uses one literal
=job-42= key in fixed mode and always sends =commit=. The provider stores the
effect and key as one SQLite row, uses =insert or ignore= and withholds the
first ACK. This covers a bounded lost-ACK/restart scenario; it has no separate
external effect, expiry, parameter-conflict check or tenant namespace. The
=fixed= mode name denotes the repair to that specific injected fault only.
The inspected sources are the [[file:../../bindings/python/qualification/job_executor/worker.py][worker]],
[[file:../../bindings/python/qualification/job_executor/test_unique_effect.py][provider/test fixture]] and [[file:../../modules/assurance/host.ss][Host preflight projection]]. The reused POO Flow boundary is
=src/modules/authorization/providers/cedar/interface.ss= and its existing
decision/effect-token projection, not a new Aitia-owned authorization module.

| Contract question | Existing mechanism / evidence boundary | Remaining obligation and owner |
|-------------------+----------------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| Is this attempt currently permitted? | POO Flow Cedar Provider and effect-token machinery exist; Aitia projects preflight requests and rechecks their currentness | Host supplies trusted facts; existing Cedar evaluates policy; Runtime enforces at use. The Aitia-to-Runtime path remains unqualified; no new local authorization engine |
| Does retry preserve logical identity? | fixed mode uses one job identity across two sequential worker processes | Aitia task-state contract and actual provider must qualify tenant/job/operation scope, persistence and concurrent attempts |
| Can the same identity change its intent? | payload is constant; provider does not compare an incoming payload with the stored payload | provider rejects inconsistent reuse; Host/Runtime bind authorized intent to dispatched bytes. Test mismatch rejection without an additional effect |
| What if the deduplication record expires? | no expiry or cleanup is modeled | provider owns retention guarantee; Aitia records retry horizon and review conditions. Test boundary/late requests and explicit reconciliation or rejection outside the guarantee |
| Is effect commit atomic with deduplication? | one SQLite row stands for both key and effect; no failure between independent commits is modeled | actual provider owns atomicity or its recovery protocol. Qualify crashes before/after commit and lost response; policy permission alone is insufficient |
| What if two attempts are allowed concurrently? | existing scenario restarts workers sequentially | Runtime/provider qualify atomic reservation or deduplication and outcome reconciliation; two Cedar permits must not be mistaken for unique execution |

Do not copy these checks into a second Python policy layer. Trusted
identity/intent, current authority and applicable retry-window conditions may
be inputs to the existing Cedar policy boundary. Their truth must come from
the responsible Host/provider, not caller-supplied booleans. Policy evaluation
does not persist a deduplication record, make a network request atomic, or
prove whether an unacknowledged attempt committed.

Retry identity and authorization identity are distinct: a logical operation
keeps its identity after restart, but an old permit need not remain valid.
An unknown result must retain that uncertainty until the provider's qualified
retry/reconciliation contract resolves it. Generic POO Flow reconciliation
cannot be assumed to establish uniqueness of an external provider effect.

The desired design remains stable logical identity with current authorization
and a qualified provider contract. The added obligations refine its conditions;
they do not silently turn the guarantee into unconditional exactly-once delivery.
